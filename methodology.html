Sources, rules, and limitations

How MOS results work

The calculator compares the information you enter with the April 2025 MOS and Line Score Wizard. It is a counseling aid, not an official Army determination.

Qualification and availability are separate

A person can meet the modeled requirements for an MOS even when no Idaho vacancy is publicly visible. Likewise, a public posting does not mean every applicant qualifies or that the vacancy remains open. Every result keeps those two questions separate.

What “not eligible yet” means

The result lists each unmet or unknown requirement. For a line score, it shows the score entered, the threshold, and the point gap. For PULHES, vision, citizenship, licensing, clearance screening, or Split Training Option rules, it names the item that needs review and gives a practical next step. Unknown information never counts as a pass.

Result labels

Eligible
All modeled requirements passed with the information entered.
Waiver scenario
The score is within a workbook-modeled non-GT waiver range. A waiver is never guaranteed.
Needs review
At least one requirement is unknown or could not be interpreted safely from the source.
Not eligible yet
At least one modeled requirement was not met. The result explains why and what to verify next.

Sources and freshness

Qualification requirements come from DA PAM 611-21 Table 10-6 MOS and Line Score Wizard (April 2025). Idaho availability labels use public evidence observed on September 4, 2026. Vacancies, policies, training seats, bonuses, and exceptions can change, so a recruiter must confirm them.

Privacy

The calculator runs in your browser. It does not request a name, Social Security number, birthday, medical documents, or detailed legal information. Saved MOSs stay in this browser’s local storage and are not transmitted by this site.

Final authority

MEPS, current Army policy, security adjudication, medical qualification, Idaho’s authorized vacancy system, and official recruiter review control the final decision.
